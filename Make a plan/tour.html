Taj Mahal Overview

Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 for being "the jewel of Islamic art in India and one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage". It is regarded as one of the best examples of Mughal architecture and a symbol of Indian history. The Taj Mahal is a major tourist attraction and attracts more than five million visitors a year. In 2007, it was declared a winner of the New 7 Wonders of the World initiative. The Taj Mahal and its setting, surrounding grounds, and structures are a Monument of National Importance, administered by the Archaeological Survey of India.

The Taj Mahal incorporates and expands on design traditions of Indo-Islamic and Mughal architecture.[19] Inspirations for the building came from Timurid and Mughal buildings including the Gur-e Amir in Samarkand (the tomb of Timur, progenitor of the Mughal dynasty) and Humayun's Tomb in Delhi which inspired the Charbagh gardens and hasht-behesht plan of the site.[20][21] The building complex employs symmetrical constructions with the usage of various shapes and symbols.[19] While the mausoleum is constructed of white marble inlaid with semi-precious stones, red sandstone was used for other buildings in the complex similar to the Mughal era buildings of the time.

Golden Temple Overview

Golden Temple

The Golden Temple[a] is a gurdwara located in Amritsar, Punjab, India.[3][4] It is the pre-eminent spiritual site of Sikhism. It is one of the holiest sites in Sikhism, alongside the Gurdwara Darbar Sahib Kartarpur in Kartarpur, and Gurdwara Janam Asthan in Nankana Sahib.[3][5] The sarovar (holy pool) on the site of the gurdwara was completed by the fourth Sikh Guru, Guru Ram Das, in 1577.[6][7] In 1604, Guru Arjan, the fifth Sikh Guru, placed a copy of the Adi Granth in the Golden Temple and was a prominent figure in its development.[3][8] The gurdwara was repeatedly rebuilt by the Sikhs after it became a target of persecution and was destroyed several times by the Mughal and invading Afghan armies.[3][5][9] Maharaja Ranjit Singh, after founding the Sikh Empire, rebuilt it in marble and copper in 1809, and overlaid the sanctum with gold leaf in 1830. This has led to the name the Golden Temple

Somnath Temple Overview

Somnath Temple

The Somnath Temple is a significant Hindu temple located in the Prabhas Kshetra near Veraval in Saurashtra, Gujarat, India. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva and is considered to be the first among them. The temple has a rich history and has been rebuilt several times after being destroyed by various invaders. The current structure was completed in 1951 and is a fine example of modern Hindu temple architecture. The temple attracts millions of devotees and tourists every year, making it a vital pilgrimage site in India.

Red Fort Overview

Red Fort

The Red Fort is a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in Delhi, India. It served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years, until 1856. The fort is an outstanding example of Indo-Islamic Mughal architecture, blending Persian, Timurid, and Indian styles. It is made of red sandstone and is surrounded by a moat. The Red Fort is a symbol of India's rich history and culture, attracting millions of visitors each year.

Qutub Minar Overview

Qutub Minar

The Qutub Minar is a UNESCO World Heritage Site located in Delhi, India. It is a minaret that forms part of the Qutub complex, which is home to several other historically significant structures. The Qutub Minar was commissioned in 1193 by Qutb al-Din Aibak, the founder of the Delhi Sultanate, to mark the beginning of Muslim rule in India. Standing at 73 meters tall, it is the tallest brick minaret in the world and is known for its intricate carvings and inscriptions. The Qutub Minar is a prime example of Indo-Islamic architecture and attracts millions of visitors each year.

Lotus Temple Overview

Lotus Temple

The Lotus Temple, located in Delhi, India, is a Bahá'í House of Worship that was completed in 1986. Notable for its flowerlike architecture, the Lotus Temple has won numerous awards and has been featured in various publications. It is open to all, regardless of religion, and is a place for meditation and reflection. The temple is surrounded by beautiful gardens and ponds, making it a serene escape from the bustling city. The Lotus Temple is one of the most visited buildings in the world, attracting millions of visitors each year.

Kodaikanal Overview

Kodaikanal

Kodaikanal is a hill station in the southern Indian state of Tamil Nadu. It is located in the Western Ghats mountain range and is known for its scenic beauty, pleasant climate, and lush greenery. The town is situated at an altitude of about 2,000 meters (6,561 feet) above sea level and is surrounded by forests of eucalyptus and pine trees. Kodaikanal is often referred to as the "Princess of Hill Stations" and is a popular tourist destination for those seeking a peaceful retreat from the heat of the plains.

The main attractions in Kodaikanal include the Kodaikanal Lake, which is a star-shaped lake surrounded by a beautiful park, and the Coaker's Walk, a scenic walkway that offers stunning views of the valleys below. Other popular spots include the Pillar Rocks, Guna Cave, and the Bryant Park. Kodaikanal is also known for its rich biodiversity, with several endemic species of flora and fauna found in the region.

Jagannath Temple Overview

Jagannath Temple

The Jagannath Temple is a famous Hindu temple located in Puri, Odisha, India. It is dedicated to Lord Jagannath, a form of Lord Krishna. The temple is known for its annual Rath Yatra (chariot festival), where the deities are taken out in massive chariots for a grand procession. The temple's architecture is a blend of Kalinga and Dravidian styles, featuring intricate carvings and a towering spire. It is one of the Char Dham pilgrimage sites and attracts millions of devotees and tourists each year.

ISKCON Temple Overview

Iskcon Temple Delhi

Sri Sri Radha Parthasarathi Mandir, generally known as the ISKCON Temple Delhi, is a Hindu temple of Krishna and Radha in the form of Radha Parthasarathi. The Temple was inaugurated on 5 April 1998 by the then Prime Minister of India, Atal Bihari Vajpayee[1] in the presence of former Chief Minister of Delhi, Sahib Singh Verma, and Sushma Swaraj. It is located at Hare Krishna Hills (near Nehru Place), in the East of Kailash area of New Delhi, India.

The temple complex houses the Glory of India Vedic Cultural Centre, a popular destination for visitors and tourists to learn about major Hindu texts which are presented using various multimedia technologies, these include: Bhagavad Gita Animatronics - Using a blend of dramatic narration, lasers, and projects, this show allows the visitors to learn the five major concepts of Bhagavad Gita, the three modes of nature, and the Yoga systems presented therein. Mahabharat Experience - A light and sound show which presents the storyline of Mahabharat which concisely spans over thousands of verses. Ramayana Art Gallery - A collection of over 30 original oil paintings painted by ISKCON's members from the USA, Russia, India, and the UK. Bhagavat Puran Exhibit - This exhibit visually presents one of the most important texts in the Vaishnava tradition.
